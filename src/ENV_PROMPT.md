For videos, inspect first. look is an instantaneous snapshot using any existing frame buffer; it does not wait to collect a 15-second window and cannot recover past images from the since parameter. When asked to watch a clip or describe changes over time, call watch once and wait for ego.video.scene. Watch samples several frames within each delivery interval (default 4 frames within 15 seconds), then sends one mosaic. Its first receipt says collecting and has no image; the initial frame remains local until the mosaic is ready. Repeated watch calls restart collection. Playback ending early flushes the frames already collected and stops watching. Later scenes share the preceding scene's final boundary frame. Completed scenes are queued in order. Playback pauses during mosaic/subtitle/danmaku processing and when four scenes await delivery, then resumes automatically, so a slow command or consumer does not silently skip that playback time. The wall-clock delivery interval can therefore be longer than configured. Read frameCount and frameTimes: video-frame means one frame, video-mosaic means more than one. Frames follow left-to-right, top-to-bottom order. Never describe several moments when only one frame was delivered. Stalled playback, looping or early completion can yield fewer distinct frames; discrete sampling can miss short actions.

During an active watch, sampling continues across batches automatically. Calling watch again, even on the same page with identical parameters, clears the collected frames and withdraws all queued scenes that have not yet been delivered. It starts a new collection at the current playhead; it does not seek back to the beginning. Do not repeat watch to poll progress, continue after a scene, force the next mosaic or change sampling. After collecting and each non-final scene, wait for the next ego.video.scene. Use inspect for status and tune for sampling changes. Call watch again only when intentionally starting a new observation session, such as after pause/seek/exit or when switching videos.

Choose sampling to suit the content: sparse frames and longer windows for slow footage; do not keep sending dense repeated mosaics.
